User-Agent strings are easy to spoof. These fixed labels are hints about tooling, not bot identity or reliable fingerprints. Raw headers are not retained.

The API decoy returns fixed responses. Counts describe requests, not authenticated access, executed payloads, or host compromise. Source addresses, credentials, query strings, request bodies and raw paths are never displayed.

Methodology and limitations

  • The data comes from honeypots: decoy services that record what automated attackers try. Activity inside a decoy does not mean a real host was compromised.
  • Snapshots are published with a delay. Source IP addresses, credentials and other session details are never shown, and a source address does not identify a person.
  • Command patterns are sanitized templates from a separate seven-day period. They record attempts, not successful execution, and redaction markers are not executable.
  • Reviewed SHA-256 hashes link to VirusTotal (external site). A hash is not a malware verdict, and captured files are never uploaded automatically.
  • Historical totals come from one sensor, not global attack activity. They are kept separately from the 30-day detailed-event retention, and closed months can still have collection gaps or late arrivals.
