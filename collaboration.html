Collaboration

I'd rather work alongside people than pitch at them. Here's what I'm most interested in building, breaking, and arguing about.

The short version: if you're working on something in the space below, I'd like to hear about it. Two engineers comparing notes on a hard problem is genuinely one of my favorite ways to spend time. Send me a message—tell me what you're building or breaking, and let's see where it goes.

Topics I'm Interested In

Not an exhaustive list, but this is where I'm most likely to be genuinely useful to you.

Primary interest

Authorization and access control

BOLA, IDOR, tenant isolation, privilege boundaries, and the general problem of proving that your access rules do what you think they do. This is the thing I care most about and the reason most of the tooling exists. If you're designing a permissions model or trying to test one, I will happily talk your ear off.

Open source

Contributing to the tools

Issues, pull requests, feature arguments, and "this design is wrong because…" are all welcome on any published repository. If you've used VardrGate or VardrMap in anger and it got in your way, that feedback is worth more to me than a star. Security issues in the tools themselves: please report those privately first.

API security

Authentication flows, token handling, rate limiting, and the OWASP API Top 10 as it actually shows up in real codebases rather than in slide decks.

Secure design review

Threat modeling a feature before it ships, or a second opinion on an architecture decision while it's still cheap to change. Earlier is always better than later.

Research and writing

Co-authoring a writeup, reviewing a draft, or digging into a vulnerability class together. I learn more from this than from almost anything else.

Also genuinely welcome: students and career changers with questions. I came into this from the Marine Corps and a fire department, not a computer science degree, and people answering my questions early on is a large part of why I got anywhere. Ask.

How I Like to Work

Whatever the shape of it, a few things hold.

Show the work

If I claim something is exploitable, I can demonstrate it. If I can't reproduce it, I won't claim it. That applies to a pull request review the same way it applies to a finding.

Authorization first

Anything touching a system I don't own happens with explicit written permission and a defined scope. No exceptions, no gray areas. It's why scope allowlists are compiled into the tools rather than left to memory.

Plain language

No severity inflation, no jargon used to sound impressive, and no pretending to certainty I don't have. If I'm guessing, I'll say I'm guessing.

Tell me what you're working on

A question about one of the tools, a permissions model you're unsure about, an idea you want to argue about, or just an introduction—all of it is welcome. I read everything that comes in.
